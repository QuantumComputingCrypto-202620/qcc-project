# vqe-lih: Report

**LiH dissociation curve with VQE: UCCSD vs a hardware-efficient ansatz, against exact
diagonalization.** Design and definitions: [SPEC.md](SPEC.md). Every number below comes from a
committed CSV in [`results/summary/`](results/summary/) and every figure from
[`results/figures/`](results/figures/); all of them are regenerated by
[`run_all.sh`](run_all.sh) from the configs in [`configs/`](configs/).

Units: energies in Hartree (Ha), bond lengths in Å. ΔE = E_VQE − E_exact, where E_exact is the
exact ground state of the *same* qubit Hamiltonian in the correct particle-number sector with
⟨S²⟩ = 0. "Chemically accurate" means ΔE < 1.6 mHa.

## 1. Verifiable claim

Measured on the 20-point LiH curve (0.8–4.0 Å, STO-3G, Jordan-Wigner, exact statevector
estimator, L-BFGS-B, warm-started outwards from 1.6 Å):

| Active space | UCCSD: X of Y points, depth Z | Best HEA: X′ points, depth Z′ | HEA in-sector accuracy, R ≤ 2.0 Å → R ≥ 2.5 Å |
|---|---|---|---|
| **(2e, 3o)**, 6 qubits (default) | **20 of 20**, depth **953** (260 two-qubit gates, 8 parameters) | **16** (reps 3), depth **45** (15 two-qubit gates, 48 parameters) | 100 % → 20 % |
| (4e, 4o), 8 qubits (week-4 addition) | **20 of 20**, depth **5177** (1583 two-qubit gates, 26 parameters) | **16** (reps 4), depth **61** (28 two-qubit gates, 80 parameters) | 100 % → 20 % |

> "The UCCSD ansatz reaches chemical accuracy at **20 of 20** points on the curve with depth
> **953**; the hardware-efficient ansatz reaches it at **16** points with depth **45** (21×
> lower), but fails near dissociation where the state becomes more strongly correlated."
> — (2e, 3o) active space. The (4e, 4o) space gives the same picture: 20/20 at depth 5177 vs
> 16/20 at depth 61.

Source: [`e4_tradeoff_claim.json`](results/summary/e4_tradeoff_claim.json),
[`e4_tradeoff.csv`](results/summary/e4_tradeoff.csv). Depth = transpiled depth on a linear
coupling map, basis {rz, sx, x, ecr}, `optimization_level=2`, seed 1234 (`qcc_common.metrics`).

**The hypothesis "HEA fails near dissociation" is confirmed, with one important
qualification.** X′ counts a point only if it is chemically accurate *and* in the correct
symmetry sector (⟨N⟩ correct and ⟨S²⟩ < 0.01). Counting energy alone, the best HEA reaches
1.6 mHa at 19/20 (2e3o) and 20/20 (4e4o) points, so on energy alone the hypothesis would be
refuted for (4e, 4o). The difference is entirely near dissociation: there the singlet and triplet
become nearly degenerate, and the HEA (which conserves neither N nor S²) converges to
spin-contaminated states with ⟨S²⟩ up to 0.62 whose energies are within 1.6 mHa of the singlet.
Such a state has the right energy but is not the ground state, so the strict count is the one
we report. Both counts are in the claim file.

## 2. Pipeline and validation

| Step | Choice |
|---|---|
| Hamiltonian | PySCF RHF / STO-3G → `FreezeCoreTransformer` → `ActiveSpaceTransformer` → Jordan-Wigner |
| (2e, 3o) | Li 1s frozen; the three σ orbitals (2σ, 3σ, 4σ) selected by symmetry (zero p_x/p_y weight), π dropped → 6 qubits, 118 Pauli terms |
| (4e, 4o) | No frozen core; the four σ orbitals → 8 qubits |
| UCCSD | qiskit-nature `UCCSD`, one Trotter step, HF initial state |
| HEA | `efficient_su2` (RY + RZ, linear CX), HF bitstring prepended, reps 1–4 |
| VQE | `qiskit_algorithms.VQE`, exact `StatevectorEstimator` (E1–E5) |

**E1, H₂ validation** ([figure](results/figures/e1_h2_full.png),
[CSV](results/summary/e1_h2.csv)). UCCSD + COBYLA from HF at 19 bond lengths, 0.3–2.5 Å: the
largest error is 4.1 × 10⁻⁷ Ha (at 2.5 Å), and the exact qubit-Hamiltonian energy equals PySCF
FCI to 10⁻¹⁵ Ha. The fast tests also check that UCCSD with zero parameters reproduces E_HF for
LiH, and the qubit and parameter counts of both ansätze.

**Active-space error is reported separately** (E_exact − E_FCI, full STO-3G FCI). For (2e, 3o)
it is 3.2 mHa at 0.8 Å, 1.25 mHa at 1.6 Å and 0.36 mHa at 4.0 Å; (4e, 4o) is only slightly
better (2.5, 1.16, 0.22 mHa). So at short bond lengths the *active space* already misses
chemical accuracy relative to full FCI, whatever the ansatz does. None of the VQE numbers in
this report include it. The correlation energy (E_HF − E_exact) grows from 19 mHa at
equilibrium to 159 mHa at 4.0 Å, which is what makes the stretched region hard.

## 3. UCCSD along the curve (E2)

Figures: [(2e, 3o)](results/figures/e2_uccsd_2e3o.png), [(4e, 4o)](results/figures/e2_uccsd_4e4o.png).

- **(2e, 3o):** ΔE ≤ 1.9 × 10⁻⁹ Ha at every point, with a median of 90 energy evaluations per
  point. As predicted in SPEC §3, two active electrons make UCCSD exact (CCSD is exact for two
  electrons), so this space is a trivial test for UCCSD.
- **(4e, 4o):** UCCSD is no longer exact. ΔE grows from 2.4 × 10⁻⁶ Ha at equilibrium to
  4.7 × 10⁻⁵ Ha at 4.0 Å, about 20×, as static correlation grows. It still stays 30× below the
  threshold. The price is depth: 5177, against 953 for (2e, 3o).
- **BeH₂ (stretch, (4e, 4o) σ space, 8 qubits)**, [figure](results/figures/beh2_4e4o.png),
  [CSV](results/summary/beh2.csv): the same UCCSD *does* break down when stretched. ΔE is 0.013,
  0.033 and 0.29 mHa at 1.0, 1.33 and 1.8 Å, then **1.8 mHa at 2.5 Å and 6.2 mHa at 3.2 Å**.
  With two bonds breaking, the stretched state is strongly multi-reference, and a single
  Trotter step of UCCSD is not enough. HEA reps 2 and 4 miss chemical accuracy everywhere on
  BeH₂ (10–158 mHa), and are in the wrong sector at 2.5 and 3.2 Å.

## 4. Hardware-efficient ansatz (E3) and the depth trade-off (E4)

Figures: [E3 (2e, 3o)](results/figures/e3_hea_2e3o.png),
[E3 (4e, 4o)](results/figures/e3_hea_4e4o.png),
[error vs depth](results/figures/e4_tradeoff_error_vs_depth_2e3o.png),
[error vs distance](results/figures/e4_tradeoff_error_vs_distance_2e3o.png) (and `_4e4o`).

| Space | Ansatz | Params | Depth | 2q gates | Accurate & in sector | Accurate (energy only) | Wrong-sector points | Median ΔE (Ha) | Max ΔE (Ha) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2e3o | UCCSD | 8 | 953 | 260 | 20 | 20 | 0 | < 10⁻⁹ | 1.9 × 10⁻⁹ |
| 2e3o | HEA r1 | 24 | 23 | 5 | 0 | 0 | 0 | 0.0197 | 0.159 |
| 2e3o | HEA r2 | 36 | 34 | 10 | 0 | 1 | 5 | 0.0041 | 0.040 |
| 2e3o | HEA r3 | 48 | 45 | 15 | **16** | 19 | 3 | 0.0002 | 0.0016 |
| 2e3o | HEA r4 | 60 | 56 | 20 | 11 | 16 | 5 | 0.0011 | 0.0023 |
| 4e4o | UCCSD | 26 | 5177 | 1583 | 20 | 20 | 0 | 3 × 10⁻⁶ | 4.7 × 10⁻⁵ |
| 4e4o | HEA r1 | 32 | 28 | 7 | 0 | 0 | 0 | 0.0198 | 0.159 |
| 4e4o | HEA r2 | 48 | 39 | 14 | 0 | 0 | 5 | 0.0029 | 0.071 |
| 4e4o | HEA r3 | 64 | 50 | 21 | 1 | 5 | 4 | 0.0033 | 0.012 |
| 4e4o | HEA r4 | 80 | 61 | 28 | **16** | 20 | 4 | 0.0003 | 0.0015 |

Observations:

- **HEA reps 1 is exactly Hartree-Fock.** At every point the optimizer stops at E_HF: 0 %
  correlation energy recovered, and ΔE equals the correlation energy, rising to 159 mHa at
  4.0 Å. A single entangling layer cannot leave the HF determinant downhill.
- **More layers do not help monotonically.** In (2e, 3o), reps 4 is worse than reps 3, and in
  (4e, 4o) reps 3 is far worse than reps 4. With 48–80 parameters, L-BFGS-B often hits the
  15,000-evaluation cap, and the warm-start chain carries a poor local minimum along the curve.
  This is an optimization problem, not an expressivity one: reps 4 contains reps 3.
- **The cost gap is large.** The best HEA is 21× (2e3o) to 85× (4e4o) shallower than UCCSD and
  uses 17× and 57× fewer two-qubit gates. UCCSD is also cheap to optimize (8–26 parameters, a
  median of 90–550 evaluations per point); the HEA needs thousands.

## 5. Optimizers and initialization (E5)

(2e, 3o), exact estimator, at 1.6, 2.5 and 3.5 Å; COBYLA, L-BFGS-B, SPSA and SPSA-small ×
{zeros, warm start, 10 random N(0, 0.1²) seeds}. Figures:
[UCCSD](results/figures/e5_optimizers_uccsd.png), [HEA r2](results/figures/e5_optimizers_hea_r2.png),
[histories](results/figures/e5_optimizers_histories.png); table:
[`e5_optimizers_stats.csv`](results/summary/e5_optimizers_stats.csv).

- **UCCSD + L-BFGS-B is robust.** It reaches ΔE ≤ 10⁻⁹ from every start at all three points,
  with a median of 81–176 evaluations. A warm start reaches 1.6 mHa on the first evaluation
  at 1.6 Å, and roughly halves the evaluation count. COBYLA usually converges (to about
  10⁻⁷ Ha) but needs a median of 110–880 evaluations, more at 3.5 Å. One of its 10 random starts
  at 3.5 Å stalls at 3.3 mHa.
- **Default SPSA often fails, and the cause is its calibration.** Qiskit's SPSA calibrates its
  step size so the first step is 2π/10 ≈ 0.63 rad, while the optimal UCCSD amplitudes here are
  about 0.05 rad. From zeros or a warm start at 1.6 Å it ends at ΔE ≈ 0.128 Ha, worse than HF.
  The calibration also divides the target step by the measured gradient, so starting *near* a
  minimum (warm start) produces especially large first steps: warm-started default SPSA fails at
  all three points. `spsa_small` uses the same calibration with a 0.05 rad target. It reaches
  ΔE ≈ 10⁻⁴ from zeros at 1.6 Å, and its warm starts succeed at 2.5 and 3.5 Å (0.68 and
  0.05 mHa). Its warm start at 1.6 Å still fails (0.128 Ha), for the same gradient-normalization
  reason. SPSA is not useful with an exact estimator, and its defaults need tuning for
  chemistry ansätze.
- **The HEA is hard to optimize from any start.** For HEA reps 2, no optimizer and
  initialization reaches 1.6 mHa at any of the three points: the best final ΔE is 3.6 mHa at
  3.5 Å, and 4.5 mHa at 1.6 Å. L-BFGS-B from zeros stops at once at a stationary point
  1.3–2.1 Ha above the ground state. With all angles zero, the CX layers map the HF bitstring
  to a different determinant (this is tested in `test_ansatze.py`). SPSA and COBYLA from random
  starts end mostly in the wrong sector (10/10 runs at 3.5 Å). The spread across 10 random
  seeds (for example 4.5 mHa to 115 mHa for L-BFGS-B at 1.6 Å) is the evidence for local
  minima.

## 6. Shot noise (E6)

UCCSD (2e, 3o) at 1.6 Å from HF, with 5 seeds per setting. Every energy evaluation measures each
of the 29 qubit-wise-commuting Pauli groups with `shots` shots. ΔE is the exact energy at the
final parameters. [Figure](results/figures/e6_shots.png),
[table](results/summary/e6_shots_stats.csv).

| Shots per group | COBYLA median ΔE | SPSA (default) median ΔE | SPSA-small median ΔE (16–84 %) | SPSA-small runs within 1.6 mHa |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 10² | 14.0 mHa | 7.0 mHa | 3.2 mHa (1.7–5.0) | 1/5 |
| 10³ | 10.1 mHa | 3.8 mHa | **0.60 mHa** (0.31–1.4) | 4/5 |
| 10⁴ | 5.5 mHa | 128 mHa | 0.25 mHa (0.11–0.41) | 5/5 |
| 10⁵ | 2.5 mHa | 127 mHa | 0.17 mHa (0.14–0.34) | 5/5 |

- **Shots needed for chemical accuracy: 10³ per group per evaluation**, about **1.9 × 10⁷
  shots in total** for one 300-iteration SPSA-small optimization (600 evaluations × 29 groups ×
  10³). This is with a well-scaled optimizer; neither default optimizer reaches chemical
  accuracy at any shot count tested.
- **COBYLA stops early under noise.** It stops after about 66 evaluations at every shot count,
  because its trust region shrinks against the noise. Its error falls with shots but is still
  2.5 mHa at 10⁵.
- Default SPSA gets *worse* as noise falls: larger calibrated steps send it into the 0.13 Ha
  basin (see E5).
- The estimator samples real shot statistics. Aer's `EstimatorV2(precision=…)` only adds the
  same Gaussian draw to the exact value on every call, so it was not used. `ShotEstimator`
  draws each group's outcomes from the exact rotated-basis distribution, and a test checks it
  against running the measured circuits on Aer.

## 7. Noise and error mitigation (E7, simulator substitute)

No IBM Quantum access was available, so E7 runs on the **FakeSherbrooke noisy simulator**
(calibrated gate, readout and T1/T2 noise) instead of hardware. Energies are evaluated at the
E2/E3 optimal parameters, transpiled to the device (`optimization_level=3`), with 10⁴ shots
per group. [Figure](results/figures/e7_noise.png), [table](results/summary/e7_noise.csv).

| Ansatz | R (Å) | Device 2q gates | Ideal ΔE | Noisy raw | + readout | ZNE (linear) | readout + ZNE (linear) | readout + ZNE (Richardson) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| UCCSD | 1.6 | 256 | 0.0 | 532 mHa | 522 | 492 | 481 | 291 |
| UCCSD | 3.5 | 256 | 0.0 | 350 mHa | 343 | 332 | 325 | 218 |
| HEA r1 | 1.6 | 5 | 19.2 mHa | 63.8 mHa | 45.2 | 40.2 | **21.1** | 20.9 |
| HEA r1 | 3.5 | 5 | 126.5 mHa | 150.3 mHa | 142.4 | 137.6 | 129.3 | 136.1 |

- **The 256-gate UCCSD circuit is essentially fully decohered.** Readout correction and ZNE
  remove at most 45 % of a 0.35–0.53 Ha error. ZNE by folding cannot extrapolate back from a
  signal that is already gone.
- **The 5-gate HEA is almost fully recovered.** Readout + ZNE brings it from 64 to 21 mHa,
  against a noiseless 19 mHa. But that noiseless error is the HF error: this ansatz cannot
  reach chemical accuracy even without noise.
- So the depth that makes UCCSD accurate is exactly what makes it unusable on current
  hardware, and the ansätze shallow enough to survive noise are not accurate enough.

## 8. Why VQE rather than QPE

- **QPE** estimates the ground-state energy to precision ε with coherent evolution of depth
  O(1/ε), using controlled-U^{2^k}. To resolve 1.6 mHa it needs total evolution time of order
  2π/ε ≈ 4 × 10³ Ha⁻¹, i.e. thousands of sequential controlled applications of the Hamiltonian evolution, each far deeper than the 256-gate UCCSD that
  already decoheres completely in E7. QPE is the fault-tolerant answer, not a near-term one.
- **VQE** keeps each circuit as shallow as the ansatz and moves the work to a classical optimizer
  and to repeated measurement. This project measures what that costs:
  - *Expressivity vs depth (E4):* the ansatz that is accurate everywhere (UCCSD) is 21–85×
    deeper than the cheapest ansatz that is accurate anywhere (HEA reps 3–4), and the shallow
    ansätze fail where correlation is strong.
  - *Optimization (E3, E5):* shallow ansätze with many parameters have local minima,
    stationary points and wrong-sector solutions; finding the ground state needs restarts and
    warm starts.
  - *Measurement (E6):* about 2 × 10⁷ shots per optimization at one geometry, consistent with
    the O(1/ε²) scaling per estimate.
  - *Noise (E7):* even at 6 qubits, the chemically motivated circuit is beyond what current
    noise and mitigation allow.
- So VQE's advantage is not accuracy. It is that it runs at all on noisy hardware, and only
  with shallow circuits. Shallow circuits recover accuracy only near equilibrium.

## 9. Limitations and decisions

- **Week-4 decision:** (2e, 3o) makes UCCSD trivially exact, so a 4-electron σ space (4e, 4o)
  was added as a second main setting. The planned (4e, 6o) space (12 qubits, 92 UCCSD
  parameters) cost about 0.5 s per energy evaluation, roughly 7 h for the curve, and was not
  run.
- **Warm-start chains propagate failures.** A HEA point that lands in a poor minimum seeds the
  next point. E3 uses the best of 5 random starts only at equilibrium.
- **E7 is a simulator substitute.** No hardware was used, and the hardware columns of the
  planned table are empty.
- **SPSA-small is a tuned variant** (one parameter: 0.05 rad target step). Default SPSA is
  reported unchanged.
- **The wrong-sector tolerance is 0.01** on |⟨N⟩ − N| and on ⟨S²⟩. No penalty-term ablation
  was run.
- **BeH₂ was run at only five geometries.**

## 10. Reproducing

```bash
uv sync --all-packages
projects/vqe-lih/run_all.sh          # ~3–4 h on 4 cores; resumable via results/raw/*.parts
uv run --package vqe-lih pytest projects/vqe-lih -m "not slow"
```

Each `results/raw/<name>.json` stores the full config, seed, git SHA, `uv.lock` hash, package
versions, optimal parameters and energy histories.
